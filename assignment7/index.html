Climbing Kilimanjaro

Mount Kilimanjaro is the tallest mountain in Africa at 19,341 feet. It sits in Tanzania and is one of the Seven Summits, but it does not require ropes or technical climbing, which is what makes it such a popular first big mountain.

Why I climbed it

I climbed Kilimanjaro with my mom. It was a trip we had talked about for a long time, and it ended up meaning a lot more than just getting to the top.

The route

We took the Lemosho Route, which starts on the western side of the mountain. It is one of the longer routes, but the extra days give your body more time to adjust to the altitude.

Packing and route

What to pack

  • Broken-in hiking boots
  • Warm sleeping bag
  • Down jacket
  • Headlamp
  • Water bottles and purification tablets
  • Sunscreen and sunglasses

Lemosho Route, day by day

  1. Londorossi Gate to Mti Mkubwa camp
  2. Mti Mkubwa to Shira 1 camp
  3. Shira 1 to Shira 2 camp
  4. Shira 2 to Barranco camp by way of Lava Tower
  5. Barranco to Karanga camp
  6. Karanga to Barafu base camp
  7. Barafu to Uhuru Peak, then down to Mweka camp
  8. Mweka camp to Mweka Gate

Summit night

Summit night starts around midnight. You climb in the dark and the cold for hours, and the only thing you can see is the headlamp of the person in front of you. It is slow, it is freezing, and your body wants to quit. But when the sun comes up over the glaciers and you finally reach Uhuru Peak, you understand why people do it. Pole pole means slowly slowly in Swahili, and it is the most important advice you will get on the whole mountain.
